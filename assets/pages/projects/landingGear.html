Aircraft Landing Gear
Overview
A retractable tricycle-type landing gear designed in SolidWorks for MECE 2310U (Concurrent Engineering and Design) at Ontario Tech University. Group 7 (six students) designed the strut, wheel and brake assembly, bay doors, and the uplock and downlock mechanisms to fit a 4 x 4 x 6 inch bay agreed with our supergroup, then built a physical prototype from a Meccano kit that mirrors the CAD motion study.
My Role
I worked on the CAD & motion simulation. The struts feature an oleo-pneumatic shock strut with a main actuator, and locking pins.
Design Requirements
  • Ground clearance, tip-back angle, take-off rotation and overturn angle
  • Structural integrity, ground stability and ground controllability
  • Low cost, maintainable and manufacturable
  • Retractable nose-gear tricycle configuration
Concept Generation and Selection
We sketched and CAD-modelled three concepts that folded and engaged differently, then compared them on reliability, ease of movement and how well they fit the bay. We chose the first concept because its downlock was very strong under load, it worked with gravity, and it was feasible to replicate in Meccano.

Locking Mechanisms
Uplock. Holds the gear fully retracted against gravity, vibration and other forces until deployment is commanded. The prototype uses an elastic and friction to hold it in place.
Downlock. Locks the gear extended so it cannot fold when the aircraft touches down. A metal catch lets it resist a significant load.
CAD Motion Simulation
The motion study shows the linkage extending and retracting the gear through its full range inside the bay, which let us find interference problems before building the prototype.

Subassemblies
  • Strut: oleo-strut mount and housing, main piston, main and secondary arms, locking pins and rings
  • Wheels and brakes: dual rims, axle, brass washers, wheel frame and angled mounting block
  • Bay doors: main and sub doors, mounting beams, four door pistons, and locking pins and rings
